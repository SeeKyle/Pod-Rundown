✂️ 0.12.53: anyone can defuse (risky)

  • Every hero can now try to defuse a spotted mine; if it fails, the mine goes off. Base odds are about 30%, or about 82% with the Sapper perk. Slow and Steady helps most (steady hands), then Techy, Steady nerves, Optimal and a little Luck; the odds are capped between 5% and 97%. A Sapper goes for nearly every spotted mine no matter how cowardly he is; non-Sappers only try when their odds look decent, and bolder heroes gamble more. Only one hero works on a mine at a time.
  • After a successful defuse, Sappers keep the mine to plant 75% of the time, others 40%; otherwise it comes apart into spare parts. New stage award: 💥 Wrong wire.

🧠 0.12.43: 20 sliders

  • Three new sliders:
    • Steady ↔ Panicky. When a boss shows up, a car alarm goes off, an ally drops nearby, the unit gets swarmed or its HP falls below 35%, panicky units may lose it: they bolt, freeze up or spray wild shots (-18 accuracy). Steady ones keep working.
    • Light-Footed ↔ Loud. Loud units set car alarms off far more often when searching and can trip one just by clanking past. Their racket also draws lonely zombies in from off-screen. Light-footed units stay quiet.
    • Out in the Open ↔ Takes Cover. Cover-minded units (more so when hurt) put the nearest rock, log or wreck between themselves and the horde, then lean out over it to shoot. Their own shots clear the cover.
  • Folded into existing sliders: Selfless↔Selfish now also covers going back for the downed. On Track↔New Plan now covers Relentless: rethinkers drop a search or revive when hit, relentless units finish it. Squander↔Preserve now decides when supplies get used: medkits, herbs and batteries early or saved for emergencies. Pack Hunter↔Lone Wolf now sets spacing: pack hunters huddle with the nearest ally, lone wolves keep room.
  • Each hero has their own take on the new sliders. Warhound and Lumber_Jack are loud, Chibi, Yumi and the Druid light-footed. Flannel_Man and his Companion take cover; the Companion panics easily, Flannel_Man and the Warhound stay steady.

🧮 How a rating is made

  • Each card's tags add points to one or more stats. Deck-level checks (below) add or subtract a little.
  • Divide number: in each stat, the second-highest deck is set to 10 and everyone else is scaled to match. A deck can go past 10 (max 15), but points above 10 count half in the group totals.
  • Groups: Engine (Mana, Cards, Consistency), Offense (Threats, Finish, Reach) and Defense (Interaction, Survival, Politics) are each out of 30. Total is out of 90.
  • Commanders score like any other card. There's no commander multiplier any more.

📐 The nine stats

  • ⚡ Mana: Ramp (lasting ×0.75, temporary ×0.5, +0.25 for putting a land out), Mana Boost, Cheat (skipping costs), Lands (only nonbasics with extra value score). Deck check: land count, how many lands enter untapped, color fixing, and a land-drop simulation.
  • 🃏 Cards: 0.25 base per card drawn, +0.75 for engines, −0.50 for "this turn only".
  • 🎯 Consistency: Tutors and card selection (scry, dig, loot), plus Hit-Rate (the odds of seeing your plan cards in your first 10 cards) and Plan B (the odds of seeing plan cards that still work without your commander).
  • 🗡️ Threats: Tall (big creatures, scaled by power and effective MV), Wide (tokens and engines), Board Power (anthems; capped at 20 boosted power so a single overrun can't explode). Deck check: threat variety.
  • 🏁 Finish (was Clock): cards that end games: bursts, extra combats, extra turns, alternate wins.
  • 🥷 Reach: evasion (flying, trample, unblockable) on your creatures, cards that give evasion, and drain/burn that skips combat.
  • ✨ Interaction: removal, counters, wipes and denial, all ×0.45. Modal cards score every mode. The first wipe in a deck scores full; later wipes count less. Deck check: does it answer creatures, artifacts, enchantments, planeswalkers, graveyards and spells?
  • 🔰 Survival: blockers, damage prevention, recursion, rebuilding and protection.
  • 🗣️ Politics: three levers, Deals (group hug, monarch, votes), Deterrents (taxes, pillowfort, punishers) and Direction (goad, redirecting). Decks that pull all three levers get a bonus; very salty decks get nudged down. Single-target removal and counterspells add a little, and the rating uses the square root of the points (0.10.1).
